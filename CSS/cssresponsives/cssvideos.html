CSS RWD Videos

In responsive web design, it is important to make sure videos are fitting properly to the layout. Videos should expand to fill the entire content area while keeping their original aspect ratio.

When a fixed width or height of a video is specified, it may cause layout issues in very large or very small screens, such as breaking page layouts, distorting the shape, or displaying black bars around the video. The black bars are called letterboxing (top and bottom), pillarboxing (left and right), and windowboxing (all sides).

So, it's important to use relative units for the width and height properties such as percentage, instead of absolute values like pixels. Absolute values restrict the video from being responsive.

Responsive Videos Using width Property

To make a video scale according to screen size, set the width property of the video to 100% and height to auto.

This makes the video take up the full width of its parent element, while keeping its aspect ratio. However, on very large screens, the video may stretch beyond its natural width and appear distorted.

Responsive Videos Using max-width Property

To prevent videos from stretching beyond their natural size, use the max-width property instead of width.

With this, the video will scale down if needed, but never scale up larger than its original size.
