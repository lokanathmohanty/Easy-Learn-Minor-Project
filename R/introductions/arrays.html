R - Arrays

Arrays in R are data objects that can store multi-dimensional data with more than two dimensions. For example, an array with dimensions (2, 3, 4) contains 4 matrices, each with 2 rows and 3 columns. Arrays store data of a single type.

Creating an Array

# Create two vectors of different lengths
vector1 <- c(5,9,3)
vector2 <- c(10,11,12,13,14,15)

# Use these vectors as input to create an array of dimension 3x3x2
result <- array(c(vector1, vector2), dim=c(3,3,2))
print(result)

Output:

, , 1

     [,1] [,2] [,3]
[1,]    5   10   13
[2,]    9   11   14
[3,]    3   12   15

, , 2

     [,1] [,2] [,3]
[1,]    5   10   13
[2,]    9   11   14
[3,]    3   12   15

Naming Rows, Columns, and Matrices

column.names <- c("COL1", "COL2", "COL3")
row.names <- c("ROW1", "ROW2", "ROW3")
matrix.names <- c("Matrix1", "Matrix2")

result <- array(c(vector1, vector2), dim=c(3,3,2),
                dimnames = list(row.names, column.names, matrix.names))
print(result)

Output:

, , Matrix1

     COL1 COL2 COL3
ROW1    5   10   13
ROW2    9   11   14
ROW3    3   12   15

, , Matrix2

     COL1 COL2 COL3
ROW1    5   10   13
ROW2    9   11   14
ROW3    3   12   15

Accessing Array Elements

# Print the third row of the second matrix
print(result[3,,2])

# Print the element at 1st row, 3rd column of the 1st matrix
print(result[1,3,1])

# Print the entire 2nd matrix
print(result[,,2])

Output:

COL1 COL2 COL3 
   3   12   15 
[1] 13
     COL1 COL2 COL3
ROW1    5   10   13
ROW2    9   11   14
ROW3    3   12   15

Manipulating Array Elements

# Create two arrays
array1 <- array(c(vector1,vector2), dim=c(3,3,2))
array2 <- array(c(vector1,vector2), dim=c(3,3,2))

# Extract matrices from arrays
matrix1 <- array1[,,2]
matrix2 <- array2[,,2]

# Add the two matrices
result <- matrix1 + matrix2
print(result)

Output:

     [,1] [,2] [,3]
[1,]   10   20   26
[2,]   18   22   28
[3,]    6   24   30

Calculations Across Array Elements

Use the apply() function to perform calculations across elements of an array. The syntax is apply(x, margin, fun), where x is the array, margin indicates dimensions, and fun is the function applied. 

# Create array
new.array <- array(c(vector1, vector2), dim=c(3,3,2))
print(new.array)

# Calculate the sum of rows across all matrices
result <- apply(new.array, c(1), sum)
print(result)

Output:

, , 1

     [,1] [,2] [,3]
[1,]    5   10   13
[2,]    9   11   14
[3,]    3   12   15

, , 2

[1,]    5   10   13
[2,]    9   11   14

[1] 56 68 60
