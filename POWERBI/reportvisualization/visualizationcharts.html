When you open Power BI Desktop, click on the Report View. Under the "Visualizations" pane, you will find a variety of charts and visuals that you can use to enhance your reports, dashboards, and data storytelling.

Types of Visuals in Power BI Desktop

  • Stacked Bar Chart: Horizontal bars stacked without overlap to show parts of a whole.
  • Stacked Column Chart: Vertical bars stacked to depict cumulative values.
  • Clustered Bar/Column Charts: Display multiple data series grouped together for comparison.
  • 100% Stacked Bar/Column Charts: Show proportions as parts of a whole (100%) using stacked bars or columns.
  • Line Chart: Ideal for showing trends over time, such as monthly sales.
  • Area Chart / Stacked Area Chart: Area under the line is filled; stacked area shows multiple series.
  • Line and Stacked Column Chart: Combines a line chart and stacked columns to compare categorical and numeric data.
  • Line and Clustered Column Chart: Similar combo chart to correlate two numeric fields.
  • Ribbon Chart: Uses ribbons to compare ranking of categories over time.
  • Waterfall Chart: Visualizes positive and negative changes in values over time.
  • Funnel Chart: Represents stages in a process and how data decreases at each stage.
  • Radial Gauge Chart: Circular gauge used to track KPIs.
  • Scatter Chart: Shows relationships between two numeric variables.
  • Pie Chart: Represents data in a circular format showing proportions.
  • Donut Chart: A variant of the pie chart with a hole in the center.
  • Treemap: Uses nested rectangles to display hierarchical data.
  • Map: Plots data points on a geographic map.
  • Filled Map: Fills regions on a map based on data.
  • Azure Map: Requires login; fetches geospatial data from Azure.
  • Card / Card (New): Displays a single numeric value, such as total sales or revenue.
  • Multi-row Card: Shows multiple values on a card, like employee salary and bonus.
  • KPI Visual: Tracks performance indicators like sales, profit, or goals.
  • Slicer: Enables filtering of data on the report page interactively.
  • Table / Matrix: Tabular display of data with sorting and formatting options.
  • R Script / Python Visual: Requires installation of R or Python. Used for advanced statistical or machine learning visuals.
  • Decomposition Tree: Breaks down data hierarchically into multiple branches.
  • Q&A Visual: Ask questions in natural language; AI returns visual answers.
  • Smart Narrative: Automatically generates a text summary of data insights.
  • Paginated Report: Pixel-perfect reports suitable for printing and PDFs.
  • ArcGIS Maps for Power BI: Geographic visuals requiring user sign-in.
  • Power Apps for Power BI: Embed and connect Power Apps inside reports.
  • Power Automate for Power BI: Adds a button to trigger automated flows.
